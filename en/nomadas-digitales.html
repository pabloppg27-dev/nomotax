Frequently asked questions

Can I be tax resident in no country at all?

It's risky: your home country may keep treating you as resident and banks will ask you for a tax residency. The safe option is to have one, well chosen and properly evidenced.

What if I spend less than 183 days in each country?

Days aren't everything: your home, your family and where your economic interests are also count. You can still be resident in your home country even if you spend little time there.

Do I need a company?

It depends on how much you invoice and where your clients are. Sometimes being self-employed in your country of residence is enough; other times a company pays off. We look at it with numbers.

Can I keep invoicing through my company back home?

Yes, but if you run it from another country, that country may consider the company resident there. We review whether it's best to keep it, move it or set up another one.

Which visa suits me?

The one that fits your nationality, how you work and where you want to pay tax. A nomad visa doesn't always make you tax resident, and vice versa: we explain it for your case.

What happens to my accounts?

Banks report your accounts to the country where you declare residency. We open the ones you need and keep your residency up to date with each bank.
